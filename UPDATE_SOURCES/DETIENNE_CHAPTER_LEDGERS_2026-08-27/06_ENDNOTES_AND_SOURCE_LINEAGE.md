# Marcel Detienne, *Comparing the Incomparable*: endnotes and source-lineage ledger

## Scope and conventions

- **Coverage:** the actual Notes occupy printed pp. 101–108, PDF pp. 116–123. PDF pp. 124–128 are a publisher's *Cultural Memory in the Present* backlist, not notes or sources used by Detienne, and are excluded from the citation ledger.
- **Keys:** every numbered note is keyed below as `chapter.note`, followed by its printed Notes page and PDF page. The body claim is identified by printed body page where useful.
- **Type flags:** **P** = primary text/evidence for the historical case at issue; **S** = modern secondary scholarship or theory; **A/S** = Detienne's own earlier scholarship or a volume he edited, therefore authorial lineage/self-citation rather than independent corroboration; **I** = internal or unpublished research document; **N** = no bibliographic source supplied. A modern edition may mediate a primary source. These flags are inferred only from the bibliographic form and the book's own framing; no external bibliographic research was used.
- **Polymyth relevance:** every such statement is a **provisional inference for follow-up**, not a ruling imported from the source.
- **Rendered-page corrections:** the ledger follows the page images where OCR was visibly corrupt, including *L'Invention de la mythologie*, *L'Identité de la France*, *L'Être et le temps*, François Jullien, Geoffrey Lloyd, F. Robert, and *Paulys Realencyclopädie der classischen Altertumswissenschaft*.

### Chapter 1 lineage and citation limits

1. **Renaissance comparativism:** Huppert and Vivanti are Detienne's main secondary gateways; La Popelinière's letter and maxim reach the book through their recovery and reproduction. Detienne expressly refuses a strict origin genealogy.
2. **Nation and discipline:** Finkielkraut, Mauss, Sternhell, Todorov, Lévi-Strauss, Furet, Bloch, Dumoulin, Nora, Elliott, Braudel, Febvre, Oexle, and Noiriel form a cumulative argument that national history, academic credentialing, and the policing of comparison developed together. Several links are interpretive or polemical rather than demonstrated by the note alone.
3. **Self-citation:** notes 1.8 and 1.28 send major historical syntheses to Detienne's earlier books. They extend the evidentiary trail but are not independent confirmation.
4. **Uncited or weakly pinned claims:** 1.2 is passim; 1.14 names Schelling without a work; 1.16's Fondation Marc-Bloch statement is unsourced; 1.23 does not transparently document the named 1998–99 episode; 1.26 is autobiographical; and the chapter's remarks about France in 1998, Nazi continuities, and national educational structures often outrun the attached references.

### Chapter 2 lineage and citation limits

1. **Research program rather than isolated essay:** notes 2.4–2.8 locate this chapter inside Detienne's collective experiments. The main source base is therefore distributed across edited volumes and collaborators.
2. **Constructed comparability:** Cohn, Valensi, Jullien/Lloyd, Lenclud, Malamoud, Braudel, Finley, Dakhlia, and Todorov supply distinct reasons to compare, but the decisive methodological claim is Detienne's synthesis.
3. **Explicit limits:** 2.3 names corpora without works; 2.4 supplies no citation; 2.6 leaves Lenclud's response bibliographically incomplete; 2.7 delegates evidence to a volume; 2.9–2.13 are pointers rather than independent corroboration; and 2.11 says the full demonstration is not yet available.
4. **Note-only polemic:** 2.2's criticism of *Annales* and scholars of national memory is stronger than the modest salt-administration example to which the marker is attached.

## Chapter 3 — “Setting a variety of regimes of historicity in perspective”

### Unnumbered research-lineage note

This is intellectual-process provenance without a bibliographic item: the chapter emerged from a comparative seminar and an open-ended collaborative enquiry.

### Chapter 3 lineage and citation limits

1. **The chapter compares operations, not stages:** Russian memory crisis, Augustinian self-distance, Jewish remembrance, Chinese divinatory historiography, Roman calendars, Turkish stelae, Mossi speech, Amerindian historicization, Greek death practices, and Japanese double writing are placed in perspective without a single evolutionary ladder.
2. **Endorsement must be tracked:** 3.14 and 3.17 are sources Detienne qualifies or criticizes. They cannot be carried forward as his settled conclusions.
3. **Access and attribution problems:** 3.3 is unpublished; 3.5 omits the author; 3.12 has no source; 3.13 contains a date contradiction; 3.11 and 3.19 assign the same pages of *Transcrire les mythologies* to different essays.
4. **Provenance pattern:** Ricoeur mediates Augustine; modern scholarship mediates most non-Western cases; primary texts and artifacts are discussed in the body but rarely cited directly in the notes. Any Polymyth use should retain this evidence distance.

### Chapter 4 lineage and citation limits

1. **Method lineage:** Hegel/Otto and Creuzer/Welcker represent individuated or metaphysical readings; Dumézil supplies relational structure, field/mode distinctions, and experimental probes; Lévi-Strauss supplies concrete-object transformations; Detienne retains those tools while criticizing their return to fixed “boss-gods.”
2. **Primary Greek evidence:** the *Iliad*, Pausanias, Aeschylus, and the Erchia sacred-law calendar directly attest some configurations. They support particular scenes and arrangements, not the entire theory of polytheism.
3. **Self-revision:** 4.14, 4.24, and 4.28 cite Detienne's own earlier work. They are essential for intellectual lineage and fuller documentation, yet they cannot count as external confirmation.
4. **Citation gaps:** 4.6 omits an author/editor; 4.15 names no documents for Gaia and Hestia; 4.19 gives no encyclopedia entry or page; 4.20 is an ambiguous back-reference; 4.24 contains an unresolved date sequence; and 4.28 covers most of the long Delphi reconstruction with one blanket pointer.
5. **Large unsupported reach:** body pp.72–78 contain many ritual, archaeological, topographic, and lexical assertions under 4.28 alone. The chapter's closing claim that every god is plural is a theoretical result, not directly established by one cited primary passage.

## Extra claims and examples that exist only, or become materially clearer, in the notes

1. Huppert receives “all credit” for the “perfect history” frame, and Vivanti/Huppert are explicitly the recovery route to La Popelinière's 1604 letter.
2. Todorov is named as the intermediary for Barrès, and Detienne calls for Todorov's account to be publicly broadcast because it explains how the Incomparable becomes embodied in the French Nation.
3. Detienne calls the history–ethnology divide a disciplinary cold war ending only in 1983 and attributes its “thick wedge” to nationalism through Furet.
4. The Marc-Bloch Foundation is described ironically as diffusing critical thought based on “the French nation,” without a supplied source for that institutional purpose.
5. Braudel's “France as diversity” is explicitly read as nostalgic nationalism; Febvre's last book and Braudel's characterization of it deepen the critique of canonical *Annales* figures.
6. Noiriel's egalitarian program is reconstructed as stronger discipline, research permissions, credentials, and peer control. The note's phrase “all this is thrown together” marks Detienne's synthesis as polemical.
7. Detienne's own EPHE center is qualified as a ten-year comparative experiment always dominated by the Greeks.
8. The chapter 2 notes reveal the full, dispersed collaborative corpus and explicitly say it was difficult to find in bookshops.
9. Malamoud's “absence of foundation” is not a failed case; it is a deliberately induced shock that generates a new question set.
10. The Erechtheus/Athena analysis carries an explicit admission that the time for a full demonstration is “not yet ripe.”

## Provisional Polymyth research agenda generated by the notes

These are inferences for possible use, not Detienne's own named Polymyth rules.

1. **Comparables ledger:** for every comparison, record the initial question, case-selection reason, constructed unit, expected similarity, discovered difference, and whether an absent feature transformed the question.
2. **Boundary-production ledger:** track who declares an object outside history, philosophy, myth, civilization, or legitimate comparison; record the credential, chair, archive, appointment process, or national mandate that gives the declaration force.
3. **Primary-distance field:** distinguish direct primary evidence, a primary quotation carried by a secondary work, modern interpretation, Detienne self-citation, an unpublished source, and an uncited proposal.
4. **Endorsement status:** mark each source as adopted, borrowed for wording, used as an example, criticized, qualified, or cited as institutional reception.
5. **Regime-of-historicity profile:** allow a story, institution, or culture to combine several forms of past, present, expectation, recurrence, genealogy, memorial obligation, and historical rupture.
6. **Tradition-transcription profile:** record oral, written, ritual, monumental, calendrical, state-authorized, adversarial, and rewritten forms of the same tradition.
7. **Relational figure model:** represent mythic figures through positions in groupings, fields of action, modes of action, objects, gestures, ritual calendars, spatial co-presence, and changing local configurations.
8. **Litmus-probe protocol:** use concrete objects or situations to reveal contrasts, then test whether the probe merely reproduces the analyst's prior model, as Detienne's five-functions warning shows.
9. **Proximity/distance discipline:** require deep cultural-network competence while preserving an explicit account of the analyst's categories and the changes comparison forces upon them.
10. **Assembly-practice model:** define political autonomy through speaking order, agenda, space, committees, turn-taking, decision rules, records, and recurrence rather than through the bare presence of a crowd or council.
